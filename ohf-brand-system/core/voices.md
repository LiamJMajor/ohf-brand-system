# Voices: who speaks when

The ecosystem speaks in two kinds of voice. People trust each for a different reason. You trust a project because it makes something you use. You trust the foundation because it wants nothing from you: no products to sell, no competitors to beat, so its praise and its criticism both mean something. Every rule here protects those two kinds of trust from each other. A project that speaks like an advocate sounds like marketing dressed up as principle. A foundation that speaks like a product stops being believable as an advocate.

| | Foundation voice | Project voice |
|---|---|---|
| Speaks about | The cause: privacy, choice, sustainability in the smart home | Its product: releases, features, how-tos, its community |
| Speaks to | The public, press, industry, contributors, funders | The people who use it, and those who might |
| Sounds like | An advocate with nothing to sell | A maker talking to its users |
| Typically says | "This research matters." "This practice is wrong." "This move advances the open home." | "Here's what's new." "Here's how to do it." "Here's what it means for you." |
| Never | Fronts a commercial push. Speaks ahead of a project. | Criticises other companies. Speaks for the foundation. |
| Trusted because | It wants nothing from you | It built the thing, and keeps building it |

## The test
Is this about a product, or about the cause? A release, a feature, a how-to, something for sale: product, project voice. Values, the wider industry, research, praise or criticism of what others are doing: cause, foundation voice. If the test does not settle it, check the cases below. If none applies, ask rather than guess.

## Cases

### Default
Projects speak in project voice; the foundation in foundation voice. A release, feature, how-to or product milestone belongs to the project that made it. Values, the ecosystem, research, and praise or criticism of industry movements, including movements that compete with our own projects, belong to the foundation. The foundation has no competitors; it advocates for outcomes.

### When project news is also foundation news
The foundation picks up a project story only when it meets at least one of:
- It affects multiple projects.
- It changes governance, funding or ownership.
- It advances a pillar in a way worth saying to press and industry rather than to users.
- It is a security incident with ecosystem implications.

### Praise
The foundation praises any movement that advances a pillar, whoever's logo is on it, including direct competitors to our projects. No special approval beyond normal editorial flow. Praising a competitor is proof of independence.

### Criticism
When the foundation calls out a negative movement, it names names; vague criticism is toothless. Naming names carries obligations. Every call-out meets the evidence bar: facts documented and public (or first-hand and publishable); the claim specific about what the actor did and which pillar it violates; conduct targeted, never people. Every named call-out requires foundation leadership sign-off. No solo call-outs, ever.

### Crisis
When a project has a crisis (security disclosure, outage, community blow-up), the project fronts it: its users, its channels, its voice. The foundation stands visibly behind it, amplifies, and takes only the press and industry angle, only when the foundation-news criteria above are met. The foundation never speaks ahead of the project about the project's own incident.

### Cross-promotion
When one project features another, each project posts in its own voice about its own users' benefit. Nobody announces on someone else's behalf.

### Commercial boundary
Promotion follows the brand licence: the project whose brand is on the product promotes it, in its own channels. Nabu Casa's Home Assistant branded hardware ships with a Home Assistant push, in Home Assistant's voice. The foundation may amplify, contextualise, and note that the work funds the mission, but never fronts a commercial push. Same for merch: whatever brand is on the item promotes it, in store or community register, not advocacy register. Foundation advocacy voice and selling never share a post.
