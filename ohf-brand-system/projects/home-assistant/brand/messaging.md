# Home Assistant: messaging

Derives from the shared core through strategy.md. Agents copy Boilerplate verbatim. Every figure traces to `truths/home-assistant/proof-points.md`.

## Key messages
One per audience in audiences.md. Drafted; confirm in interview.

### People running it at home
Every monthly release makes the home you already have do more, without a subscription and without your data leaving the house. When something changes underneath you, the release post says so first, in its own section, so you can update with confidence.
(Answers the objection: fear of breakage on update.)

### People deciding to try it
You can start with one device and a Raspberry Pi, and grow from there at your own pace. It works with over a thousand brands, and nothing leaves your home unless you decide it should. Over two million households already run it.
(Answers the objection: complexity of set-up and the learning curve.)

### Contributors and integration developers
Home Assistant is the top open source project on GitHub by contributors, and most of them started with one device they wanted to work. The architecture is modular so adding device support is a contained task, and the developer portal shows where to start.
(Answers the objection: the project feels too big to contribute to, and it is unclear where to start.)

## Boilerplate
### One line
Open source home automation that puts local control and privacy first.

### 50 words
Home Assistant is open source home automation that puts local control and privacy first. It runs on hardware in your home, works with over a thousand brands through more than 1500 integrations, and is built by a worldwide community of tinkerers and DIY enthusiasts. Home Assistant is a project of the Open Home Foundation.

### 100 words
Home Assistant is open source home automation that puts local control and privacy first. It runs on hardware in your home, so your data stays there unless you decide otherwise, and cloud services are optional. It works with over a thousand brands through more than 1500 integrations, and ships a new release every month. Created in 2013 by Paulus Schoutsen and built by a worldwide community of tinkerers and DIY enthusiasts, it is trusted by over two million households and was the top open source project on GitHub by contributors. Home Assistant is a project of the Open Home Foundation, the non-profit that fights for privacy, choice, and sustainability in the smart home.

## We don't say
| Avoid | Say instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| seamless, robust, powerful | the specific thing | they describe nothing. The list holds for headings too (interview 2026-09-14). The home page's "Powerful automations" and the 2026.7 heading are legacy; new copy says what the automations let you do |
| click | select | works across touch and mouse (https://developers.home-assistant.io/docs/documenting/general-style-guide) |
| e.g. | for example, such as, like | docs style guide (https://developers.home-assistant.io/docs/documenting/general-style-guide) |
| master/slave | controller/device, client/server | docs style guide (https://developers.home-assistant.io/docs/documenting/general-style-guide) |
| a competitor named critically | nothing; route to foundation voice | project voices do not criticise (core/voices.md) |
| HA, HASS, Hass.io | Home Assistant | project names are never shortened (interview 2026-09-14); Hass.io was retired in the January 2020 rename (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Assistant) |
