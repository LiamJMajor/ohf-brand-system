# 2026.7: Automations that speak your language

## Context
Monthly stable release post signed by Frenck, with @piitaya, @sairon and @agners credited for helping write it. Medium impact. Candidate example proposed by onboard-project; awaiting the marketing team's confirmation.

## The work
Excerpt. Full post at the source URL.

Opening: "Home Assistant 2026.7! 🎉" then "This is one of those releases I've been looking forward to for a long time."

Headings: "Creating automations just became easier, natural and more powerful", "Activity logbook", "Update all", then the fixed closing order: Integrations, Other noteworthy changes, Patch releases, Need help, Backward-incompatible changes, All changes.

Credit: "A huge thank you to all the contributors who made this release possible! And a special shout-out to @piitaya, @sairon, and @agners who helped write the release notes this release."

## Why it works
- The author's own anticipation is the hook. It is personal and it tells the reader which section matters most.
- The automation change is framed as intent: "start from what you actually want your home to do." That is the "say what it means for you" rule in one sentence.
- Credit goes to the writers as well as the coders, which says the post is community work too.
- The closing order is identical to 2026.8 and 2026.9, so readers know where breaking changes live.

## What to copy
- One headline change, named in the first lines.
- Intent-first framing for any feature that changes how people build things.
- Credit the people who wrote, not only the people who coded.

## What not to copy
- The heading "easier, natural and more powerful". Banned word, and the list holds for headings (interview 2026-09-14). Filed as the near-miss `2026-07-near-miss-powerful-heading.md`.
- Nothing about the opening: "Home Assistant 2026.7! 🎉" is house style.
