# Open Home Foundation: voice

Inherits `core/voices.md` (foundation voice). The rules below are candidates drafted on 2026-09-15 from the foundation's own blog and home page. Each has a real Yes from published work and a No constructed for contrast. Nothing here is confirmed until the interview.

## Sounds like
| | Open Home Foundation |
|---|---|
| Speaks about | the cause: privacy, choice, and sustainability in the smart home; the industry's behaviour; its own structure, funding and events; the ecosystem it stewards |
| Speaks to | the public and press, the smart home industry, regulators, contributors, and the community that shows up |
| Sounds like | an advocate with nothing to sell; plain, warm, and willing to name who is doing what |
| Typically says | "we're no ordinary foundation." "Google gatekept key Android features." "Pull up a chair." "Share only what you want. With whom you want." |
| Never | Asks anyone to buy anything. Speaks ahead of a project about that project's news. Softens its own language to sound respectable |

## Rules

### Name the company and the conduct (draft)
When the foundation calls out a negative movement, it says who did what. Because vague criticism is toothless and naming is the whole point of an advocate with nothing to sell (`core/voices.md`, Criticism). Carries the obligations in `core/escalation.md`: evidence bar met, conduct targeted not people, leadership sign-off.
- Yes: "Google gatekept key Android features, and the European Commission asked what we thought about it. Now Alphabet must open 11 Android features to all." (https://www.openhomefoundation.org/blog/a-big-win-for-android-interoperability/)
- No: "Some large platform vendors have historically restricted interoperability in ways that harm consumers."

### Second person, and what you get to do (draft)
Address the reader directly and frame each principle as something they do, not something we believe. Because the pillars are written this way everywhere the foundation speaks first.
- Yes: "Control your personal data. Share only what you want. With whom you want." (https://www.openhomefoundation.org/)
- No: "The foundation is committed to user data sovereignty and granular consent."

### Show us doing it, not believing it (draft)
Prefer a thing the foundation actually did over a statement of values. Because the foundation's credibility is behavioural: it runs its own analytics on open source software with no tracking, and published how.
- Yes: "Find out how and why we're implementing analytics on our websites: via open source software with no personal data collection or tracking." (https://www.openhomefoundation.org/blog/making-our-web-analytics-open-source-with-plausible/)
- No: "We take your privacy seriously and are committed to transparency."

### Warm, not corporate, at the door (draft)
Openings invite rather than announce. Because IFA, Community Day and the annual report all open with an invitation, and the foundation's whole argument is that this is a home, not a vendor.
- Yes: "Pull up a chair, because the foundation's 2025 Annual Report is here and packed with a year's worth of growth, milestones, and building with our community." (https://www.openhomefoundation.org/blog/the-first-of-many-our-2025-annual-report/)
- No: "The Open Home Foundation is pleased to announce the publication of its 2025 Annual Report."

## Never
- Softens its own language to sound more respectable. Strong language is permitted wherever it is earned; see `brand/style.md`, Strong language (interview 2026-09-15). Sanitising the foundation's register concedes the argument it is making.
- Asks anyone to buy anything. The foundation may name the hardware it ran, built or tested on; the moment there is a price, a store link or a launch claim, it is the project's post (`core/escalation.md`, revised 2026-09-15).
- Speaks ahead of a project about that project's own news (`core/voices.md`, Crisis and Cross-promotion).
