# ESPHome 2026.9.0

## Context
Monthly stable release post, published 16 September 2026 by Jesse Hills. Medium impact: a .0 release with foundational platform work and one user-facing security feature. Harvested during ESPHome's onboarding as the strongest available example of the accessible register, and filed with a deviation called out below. Candidate example proposed by `onboard-project`; awaiting confirmation.

## The work
Excerpt. Full post at the source URL.

Opening section, in full down to the note:

> ## Short on time? Here's the quick version
>
> Here's the plain-language version for anyone short on time or newer to ESPHome – the full technical writeup continues below. Most setups won't need any changes, but a few will.
>
> - **Modbus Controller users:** If your config uses `modbus_controller` with `custom_command`, `register_count`, `force_new_range`, or `skip_updates`, those options have been renamed or replaced and your config needs updating.
> - **ESP32 devices with a custom MAC:** If you've burned a custom MAC into an ESP32 and use Ethernet, Bluetooth, or Thread/Zigbee, those interfaces will get new addresses after updating [...]
> - **Certain UART sensors:** A handful of components (including `mhz19`, `daly_bms`, and `teleinfo`) now reject unsupported UART settings at config time [...]
>
> **New features:** Updates are quicker – ESPHome now downloads and sets up its build tools in parallel – and can be encrypted using the same key that already secures the link to Home Assistant.
>
> If you're running a typical config, aside from the items above there's nothing else you need to check before updating.
>
> Keep reading below for the full release overview and technical detail – especially if you write custom lambdas, use external components, or run a non-standard setup.

Structure: the quick version, components added, release overview, an eleven-item upgrade checklist, then roughly twenty technical sections, contributors, breaking changes, full changelog. 275 pull requests from over 40 contributors.

## Why it works
- The post is written for two readers and says so. The plain-language section is named for its reader ("short on time or newer to ESPHome") and the handoff names the other one ("if you write custom lambdas, use external components, or run a non-standard setup"). Nobody has to guess which parts are for them.
- "If you're running a typical config, aside from the items above there's nothing else you need to check before updating" is the most useful sentence in the post. It gives the majority of readers permission to stop reading, which is the opposite of what a release post usually tries to do.
- The three summary items are the change-note register translated, not diluted. They still name `modbus_controller`, `mhz19`, `daly_bms`, but each one is framed as a condition the reader can check against their own file: "If your config uses X".
- Consequences are traced past the software into the reader's house: the custom MAC item explains that DHCP reservations, Bluetooth pairings and network filters keyed to the old address need updating too.
- The new feature is described by what it costs the reader, not what was built: updates are quicker, and can be encrypted "using the same key that already secures the link to Home Assistant" rather than by naming ChaCha20-Poly1305 at that point.
- Contributors are credited by handle with pull request numbers, holding the rule confirmed on 2026-09-16.

## What to copy
- The two-reader structure: plain-language summary first, naming its reader; technical write-up after, naming its reader.
- Telling most readers there is nothing further to check. The sentence only works because the exceptions were named first.
- Conditional framing in change notes: "If your config uses X, then Y", so the reader tests it against their own file rather than parsing a changelog.
- Following a change out into its consequences, where those land outside ESPHome.
- Describing a security feature by what it protects and what key it reuses, before the algorithm.

## What not to copy
- **The heading case. This post is in sentence case throughout and the rule is Title Case** (`brand/style.md`). "Release overview", "Upgrade checklist" and "Thank you, contributors" here should be "Release Overview", "Upgrade Checklist" and "Thank You, Contributors". Ruled on 2026-09-17 that the post deviates from the rule rather than changing it. Copy the structure and the register from this example; take heading case from `brand/style.md`.
- The en dashes. Banned in every register across every project and the foundation (`decisions/2026-09-15-social-register-and-tells.md`, confirmed org-wide 2026-09-16). This post uses them freely; the quoted excerpt above preserves them because it is a quotation.
- The "Short on time?" section as a required heading. It demonstrates the direction, which is lowering the technical language in ESPHome's communications to make it easier to understand and adopt. It is not yet a fixed part of the structure (ruling 2026-09-17).
- The specific three-item summary length. Three items matched this release because three changes affected typical configurations. The count follows the release, not a template.
