# 2026.8: Approachable by design

## Context
Monthly stable release post by Ashley, Technical Program Manager at the Open Home Foundation, with Frenck and Bram assisting. Medium impact. Around 8,500 words. Candidate example proposed by onboard-project; awaiting the marketing team's confirmation.

## The work
Excerpt. Full post at the source URL.

Opening: "Home Assistant 2026.8! 🎉 Hi, I'm Ashley, the Technical Program Manager at the Open Home Foundation."

Theme sentence: "If there's one thread tying this release together, it's a direction we care about." Then: "The more people who can use Home Assistant, whatever their technical ability, the better."

Headings include: "The same Home Assistant, minus the magic number", "Less 'advanced', more approachable", "Your entity IDs, your choice", "We can't stop adding triggers and conditions". Closing order: Integrations, Other noteworthy changes, Patch releases, Need help, Backward-incompatible changes, All changes.

Credit example: "Thanks to @klejejs for this redesign!"

## Why it works
- One theme, named early, and every section serves it. A reader can say what the release was about in a sentence.
- A named author with a role. The post has a voice because a person owns it.
- Headings carry a point of view ("Your entity IDs, your choice") rather than labelling a feature. They read as benefits without saying "benefit".
- Thanks are specific and linked, inside the relevant section.

## What to copy
- Find the thread first; if there is none, say so plainly rather than inventing one.
- Author introduction when the author changes or is new to readers.
- Headings that state the outcome or the opinion.
- Same fixed closing order as every release post.

## What not to copy
- The exclamation marks beyond the opening line. "Home Assistant 2026.8! 🎉" is house style for every release post; the enthusiasm after it belongs to this author's introduction.
- Length. 8,500 words is at the upper bound; the structure carries it, not the word count.
