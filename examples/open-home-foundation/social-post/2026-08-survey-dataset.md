# Who actually lives in a smart home?

## Context
Social post promoting the publication of the anonymised Home Assistant community survey dataset. Supplied by @liam on 2026-09-15.

A foundation-voice post about a Home Assistant dataset. It clears the foundation-news criteria in `core/voices.md` on the pillar test: publishing an open, anonymised dataset is a privacy argument aimed at the public and the industry, not a product update aimed at users.

## The work
> Who actually lives in a smart home? We asked and today the answers are yours! 🎉
>
> The anonymized results of our first Home Assistant community survey are now free for everyone: a rich dataset published openly for anyone to check, build on, and benefit from.
>
> Read on 👇

## Why it works
- Opens on a question the reader has some stake in, then answers it in the same line. No setup.
- "the answers are yours" is the whole privacy argument compressed. The data is about the community and it goes back to the community.
- "check, build on, and benefit from" names three things you may do. "Check" comes first, which is the confident move: it invites scrutiny before it claims usefulness.
- "free for everyone" and "published openly" do the arguing. There is no sentence about the foundation's values anywhere in the post.
- Serial comma in the list of three, consistent with house style.

## What to copy
- Open on a question the audience already has, and answer it immediately.
- Give the reader the thing, rather than announcing that a thing exists.
- Let the artefact carry the principle. No values statements.
- Invite scrutiny first when publishing data or research.

## What not to copy
- The question opening is strong and would go stale used weekly. It earns its place when the post genuinely answers something people wonder about.
